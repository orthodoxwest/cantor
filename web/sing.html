    How to read the bars under the score

    Under each line of the score, a short bar shows the pitch you held on each note, against a line for the note itself: above it is sharp, below it flat. Tap a note, or the bars under it, to choose its phrase, or go from mark to mark with ‹ and ›. Then Compare plays the guide there and then you; Guide and Yours play each alone, once, and Repeat plays the guide over and over. While your take plays, a line crosses the bars where it is; tap the bars of what it is playing to play on from there. Beside a long chant, a strip in the bar has a segment for each phrase, or for a few together: tap one, or drag along them, to go there. Your take before of the same chant on this device is dotted under yours. With a phrase chosen, Sing again sings just that phrase, in the key you sang it in, and shows it over your take, whose bars there are dotted under it; a phrase that holds part of a mark or phrase ending of your take is sung again with the whole of it. Another phrase sung again takes the place of the last, and your take is shown again where the last was sung and this one was not. Beside a psalm sung to a tone, a table under the score has a row for each verse and a column for each part of the tone, its flex, mediant and termination, and says how each part ended, and any ending off pitch earlier in it, so that the same fault at every mediant reads down its column; tap one to choose that part of its verse. Where the chant has a recording, Recording plays it over the phrase chosen, or the whole chant from its first note, once, in the key it was sung in; where it has been sung in a voice made from a cantor's, Sung plays that the same way, at the speed and in the key it was made at.
