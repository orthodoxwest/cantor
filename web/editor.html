    A short guide

    GABC in six steps

    GABC is the plain-text way of writing square notation that Gregorio and GregoBase use, and that every score in this site's library is written in. You write the words, and after each syllable, in brackets, the notes it is sung to. Each example below is engraved as written; Try it puts it in the editor (undo brings back what you had).

    1. Words, then notes

    Each syllable is followed by its notes in brackets. A space between syllables ends a word. The first bracket of the score holds the clef.

    2. Pitches are letters

    The letters a to m are the lines and spaces of the staff, from below the bottom line upward: d is the bottom line, e the space above it, and so on to j on the top line. The clef says where do sits: c4 puts it on the fourth line (so h is do), c3 on the third, and f3 puts fa on the third.

    3. Several notes, one syllable

    Letters written together in one bracket make a neume: they are sung on the one syllable and drawn joined. Rising notes stack (a podatus), falling ones step down (a clivis), and longer groups follow the same way. A space or a / inside the bracket separates the notes a little.

    4. Bars and breath

    Bars go in their own brackets: (,) a quarter bar for a breath, (;) a half bar, (:) a full bar at the end of a phrase, and (::) the double bar at the end of a piece. A * in the words marks where the choir takes over.

    5. Marks on the notes

    A sign after a letter changes how it is sung or drawn: . a dot that doubles the note, _ an episema that lengthens it a little, v a virga (a note with a stem), w a quilisma, ~ a liquescent for a soft consonant, s a stropha. A letter and x put a flat on that line or space: (ixi) sings ti flat.

    6. The header

    A file can start with lines that describe it, each key:value;, ended by a line holding only %%. name names the piece and mode its mode, which is printed over the initial. The editor engraves a score with or without one.
